1. Making Appointments
            One of the main essential responsibilities of the office professionals is to
             make appointments for their managers, so they should have the ability to
             schedule the manager's time exactly as he or she want it scheduled.


            The main responsibilities concerning the manger's appointments are:


               1. To keep track of all appointments so one is not overlooked


               2. To keep both your manager's appointment calendar and your calendar
                     up to the minute to avoid conflicts in scheduling

     1.1. KEEPING AN APPOINTMENT SCHEDUAL:
         Since both managers and their office professionals make appointments, the
          trick with scheduling is to keep the appointment calendar up to the minute to
          avoid conflict. Being by learning:
                   What your manager's preferences are for scheduling appointments?
                   Which appointments should be given priority?
                   How much time appointments should take?
         Your best guides for scheduling appointments will be your knowledge of your
          manager's work habits and your awareness of who his or her business
          associates and friends are.


     1.2. MAKING APPOINTMENTS BY TELEPHONE OR E-MAIL:
         Make sure both you and the person seeking the appointment understand the
          correct time, date, and location. While you are talking on the telephone, repeat
          the date, day, and time and write them down or enter them into your calendar
          immediately to reduce the chance of a mistake.
         If your manager has to approve all appointment, tell the caller you must
          confirm the appointment, and you will let the caller know if the appointment
          has been set as soon as you can check with the manager.
     1.3. USING AN ELECTRONIC CALENDAR:
         If your organization has a fully integrated electronic network, you will be able

                 -        How to Set Up Appointments:
               1. Leave Monday mornings free to start on plans for the week and to
                     handle the mail that has accumulated during the weekend.
               2. Schedule no appointments for your manager on his or her first day
                     back after being out of the office for several days.
               3. Avoid crowding your manager's schedule with appointments the day
                     before he or she leaves on a trip. Preparation for the trip has priority.
               4. Schedule appointments so they do not overlap.
               5. Be aware of top-priority meetings and allow plenty of time for them.
               6. Avoid scheduling a top-priority meeting immediately after one of
                     equal significance.
               7. Avoid scheduling an appointment for your manager in another location
                     immediately after a meeting that is likely to run overtime.
               8. Schedule unstructured time frames of ten fifteen minutes between
                     appointments, giving your manager a chance to make telephone calls,
                     sign letters, think about the next conference or just take a break.
               9. At the time someone in requesting an appointment by telephone,
                     suggest specific times, preferably giving the person a chance to select a
                     time from at least two choices.
               10. When you are arranging an appointment for a short period, let the
                     person know the length of the appointment. If you are setting up the
                     appointment for 2 p.m. say tactfully, "from 2 until 2:15."
               11. When you arrange for an unexpected visitor to see your manger, let the
                     visitor know the next appointment is within ten minutes if it truly is.

     1.5. MAKING ENTRIES IN CALENDARS:
              All appointments made by telephone, by e-mail, by letter, and in person—
               including those appointments your manager makes—should be entered in
               both the manager's and your appointment calendars.
              Be consistent and prompt in recording all appointments and commitments
               in your manager's calendar. To do this, adopt a systematic plan for making
               the entries and then do not deviate from your plan.


     1.6. CANCELING APPOINTMENTS:
              When someone calls to cancel an appointment, always offer to schedule
               another one. Be sure to free both appointment calendars, if you keep two
               calendars, of the canceled appointment.
              When you must cancel an appointment for your manager, let the person
               whose appointment is being canceled know at once. Get in touch with the
               person by telephone or e-mail.
              Use the following guidelines for canceling an appointment:
                     o Express regret on your manager's behalf.
                     o Mention the appointment must be changed.

      2.1. GREETING VISITORS:
               Visitors are influenced by their first impressions of the office and the
                office professional.
               A favorable first impression and your courteous efforts to make visitors,
                feel welcome create a receptive climate in which your manager can
                develop and maintain a good rapport with others.
               Your job is to make all visitors, whether they have appointments or not,
                feel at ease.
           Introductions
                      Here are some easy guidelines for making introductions in the office.
                       In this example, Mrs. Kensington is your manager.
                      Gender and age have nothing to do with whose name you state first. In
                       a business relationship, state the name of the person you want to honor
                       first.
                      Make the introduction sound natural. Just state their names. For
                       example, "Mrs. Kensington, this is Mr. Adams." Or simply, Mrs.
                       Kensington… Mr. Adams. "Reserve" May I introduce" or "May I
                       present" for formal affairs and for very distinguished people. You can
                       add the person's business after his or her name. For example, "Mrs.
                       Kensington, this is Mr. Adams of Great Lakes Environ Tech." Or you
                       can just use the visitor's name and hand the visitor's business card to
                       your manager.

     2.2. ATTENDING TO THE VISITOR WHO HAS AN APPOINTMENT
                A visitor is a guest; therefore, it is your job to make the visitor feel
                 welcome. Here are some guidelines to follow:
     a) Make the Visitor Feel Comfortable
          Indicate where to leave his or her coat and escort the visitor to your managers
          of-face. Pronounce both the visitor's name and your manager's name distinctly
          it the caller to go in. if appropriate, you may offer the visitor a beverage or ask
          your manager if anything is needed before you leave
     b) Invite Visitor Who Must Wait To Take a Seat.
           Keep current manager, the morning paper, and other interesting reading
          material nearby so callers who must wait for any length of time can read,
     c) Attend to Early Arrivals
          A visitor who arrives early expects to wait until the time for the appointment.
          However, when your manager is free, tell him or her immediately the visitor
          has arrived and ask if he or she is ready to see the visitor.
     d) Do Not Keep a Visitor Who Has an Appointment Waiting
          The visitor's time is valuable too, and asking the visitor to wait is
          inconsiderate. Nevertheless, manager who has numerous appointments in one
          day may have difficulty keeping to an appointment schedule. When a delay
          occurs, assure the visitor the wait will not be long. When you say, "Mr.
          Ahmed” should be available in a few minutes, “you can sound reassuring.

     e) Be Cautious About How you State the Reasons for a Delay
          You can apologize and say all appointment are running behind schedule, but
          do not explain why. Avoid statements referring to important business,
          problem, or inefficiency
     f) Do Not Forget About a Visitor
          If a visitor whose appointment is delayed cannot wait any longer and tells you
          he or she must leave, offer to make another appointment and volunteer that
          your manager will call.

2.3. ATTENDING TO STAFF VISITORS
         A critical aspect of every manager's job is maintaining two-way
          communication with the employees who report directly to him or her.
         Arrange for employees who report to your manager to meet in your manager's
          office or in a conference room designated by your manager.


2.4. TERMINATING MEETINGS
    Arrange with your manager how you are to assist in terminating meeting. Be sure
    to follow your manager's preference.
    On those days when your manager's appointment schedule is crowded, find out if
    your manager wishes you to aid in terminating appointments of visitors who
    overstay their time.


2.5. INTERRUPTING A MEETING
    Most managers discourage interruptions while they are conferring with someone.
    You must arrive at a definite understanding with your manager concerning what
    conditions are important enough to justify interrupting during a meeting.
    Clearly establish the method your manager prefers you to follow when you must
    deliver an urgent message. The following method is frequently used; the assistant
    types the message, quietly enters the manager's office, delivers the message or
    places it face down on the desk, and leaves unless an immediate answer is
    necessary.

2.6. ATTENDING TO UNSCHEDULED VISITORS
    Be just as pleasant and friendly toward the unexpected visitor as you are toward
    the visitor who has an appointment. As soon as you greet the visitor, the visitor
    probably will tell you his or her name, present a business card, and state the
    purpose of the visit. listen carefully and decide what to do.


2.7. REFUSING APPOINTMENTS
    Simply state focus the discussion on future arrangements. Handle telephone
    requests and unexpected office visitor in the same way.


  3. Managing Diverse Situations
     What can you do when a visitor is aggressive or rude? what about the visitor who
     has a language barrier or a special need? Every office professional may experience
     meeting these challenging visitor or clients from time to time. some situations are
     undesirable, but some are not. As an office professional, you should learn how to
     handle these diverse situations so they will be viewed as benefits to the company,
     the visitor, and yourself.


     3.1. VISITORS AND LANGUAGE BARRIERS
     Encounters with visitors can be challenging if they do not speak your language,
     and you do not speak theirs. because of our diverse culture, today many
     companies have prepared list of bilingual employees who can interpret for
     someone or translate documents. it is quite common for these employees to be
     paid more for these services.


     3.2. VISITORS WITH SPECIAL NEEDS
        Be observant of visitors with special needs, and make them as comfortable as
        possible. if appropriate, offer to help; don't wait to be asked.

     3.3. COMPLANING CUSTOMERS
           When dealing with unhappy customers, these simple principles will help you
           stay objective and better enable you to successfully resolve any problem:
                Don’t get emotionally involved in the problem.
                Don't get defensive or aggressive- if never works.
                Provide solutions, not excuses.
                Customers are not always right, but don't tell them this.
                All customers have a right to be heard.
                Allowing a customers to abuse you is not acceptable.
                Promising action without action will lead to a customer service
                   disaster
                Sorry without corrective action is just an empty word.
                Preventing problem is easier than resolving them.

     4. Office Security
     you should be aware of any procedures established to safeguard your own
     personal security within the office setting. no one should take security for granted.
     Many office buildings have security twenty-four hours a day. if this is the case
     and you feel threatened, you should call security to have an individual removed.


    6. Hosting International Visitor
    If your manager travels to meet international clients, it's very possible those clients
    will also visit your office (see figure 10-8). Remember, the experiences and
    customs of international visitors are different than yours. Here are some tips for
    your success:
          1. Do your homework! the comfort of your guests should be your top priority.
          2. learn a few words and phrases in the visitor's language.
          3. locate the client's nearest consulate's office.
          4. keep an open mind.
          5. listen carefully
          6. Research cultural attitudes about time.
          7. learn the preferred eating habits of the country.
          8. Determine if gender plays a strong role in the client's than our own.
          9. Identify the proper greetings.
          10. Be aware of body language
          11. Pleasantries come before business.
          12. learn to pronounce names correctly.
          13. Determine if physical space when talking is different from our culture.
