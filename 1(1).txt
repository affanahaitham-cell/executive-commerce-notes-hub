1-         Introduction:


      1-1             OVERVIEW OF A SYSTEM


 An information system details how an organization plans, develops, and organizes its
 information. Because information is recorded in many different ways, think about how
 organization must handle the following: customer record, invoice, meeting notes from
 board meetings, emails, inventories, and personnel information. Maintaining this
 information in an organized and secured manner is essential to organization so it is
 useful and immediately available to the person requesting the information at the right
 time.


      1-2             WHY IS RECORED MANAGEMENT IMPORTANT:
 Effective record management ensure that the information needed is retrievable, authentic,
 and accurate. An effective record management system require an organization to:
      Set and follow policies
      Identify who is responsible and accountable for managing record.
      Create, communicate, and follow procedures consistently throughout all
         department.


      1-3             WHO IS RESPONSIBLE FOR MANAGING RECORD
           AND INFORMATION??
 Every employee is responsible. Everyone plays an important role in protecting the
 organization's record and information by following its established policies and executing
 its procedures. Large organization, the record manger supervises all the organization
 files. He or she also determines how the files will be maintained and who will maintain
 them, how long each record is to be kept, and when a record is to be removed to an
 inactive area or made ready for more permanent storage or destruction.
      1-4             WHAT ARE RECOREDS?
 Records are the evidence of what the organization does. They represent its business
 activities and decision or transaction, such as correspondence and financial statement.

 Record appears in many formats:

          Physical paper such as letters, and contracts;
          Electronic mail;
          Databases.

     1-5             CATEGORIES OF RECORD

Determining the categories of record helps an office professional know whether a record
is to be kept, where it is to be stored, how it will be stored, and how long it will be
retained. For example, paper or electronic advertisement, requests, and inquiries have or
no future use or value to an organization and should not be kept.

     A. Vital Record
     Vital records include records that are essential to the operation of the organization
     or to the continuation or resumption of operation; the recreation of the legal or
     financial status of the organization; or the fulfillment of its obligations to
     stockholders and employees in the event of a disaster.

     B. Important Record
               Important records, such as customer and inventory record, are meaningful
                to the business operation but must be limited as to the length of time they are
                retained.

     C. Useful Record
               Useful records, are document, such as correspondence and reports that are
                needed to conduct the daily business operation.

     D. Nonessential Record
               Nonessential records, are not needed beyond their current use and should be
                discarded after their use because they cost money in space, equipment, and
                employee time.


     1-6             FILES MANAGEMENT

Files management, applies record management principle to both paper and electronic
record created and used by a single office. Files management ensures that record can be
retrieved efficiently when needed.

         3. Indexing and Alphabetizing Rules
              Standardization of alphabetic filing rules is essential because it allows filing to be
              consistent and efficient. The Association of Records Managers and Administrators
              (ARMA) recognized the need for standardization and so published the first rules for
              alphabetic filing


              3-1 ORDER OF FILING UNITS
        Let's study the following terms:

     1. Unit
        Each part of a name that is used to determine the filing order is called a unit.
         For example, the name (B. R. Grove) has three units: (B., R., Grove.) The business name
         (Wacky Wig Boutique) has three units: (Wacky, wig, Boutique.)

     2. Indexing
        The process of arranging units of a name in order for filing purposes is referred to as
         indexing, as defined earlier. An example of indexing is where (B. R. Grove) is indexed
         as     (Grove      B.      R.)     Indexing     always     precedes      alphabetizing.

     3. alphabetic Filing
        Placing names in an AtoZ sequence is considered alphabetizing. For example, placing the
         name (Adamson) before the name (Bolton) is alphabetizing.

         3-2 GENERAL ALPHABETIC INDEXING PRINCIPLES
         Following the four general principles will help you to apply the filing rules:-

1.       Alphabetize by comparing names unit by unit and letter by letter. For example, in
         comparing the following indexed names, you would need to make the distinction in the
         third unit because the first and second units are identical.
         Unit 1                    Unit 2               Unit 3
         Black                     Jeff                 Peter
         Black                     Jeff                 Robert

 2.      Nothing comes before something. You would file a folder labeled Ross William first
         since the first two units are identical and Ross William does not have a third unit.
         Nothing is filed before something.
         Unit 1                    Unit 2                 Unit 3
         Ross                      William
         Ross                      William                Robert
         Another example of this principle would be:
         Unit 1                    Unit 2              Unit 3
         Chatham                   Bus                 Depot
         Chatham                   Business            College

     3-3 ALPHABETIC FILING RULES

     Learning to apply the following rules will help you to file all your paper records in nearly
     all situations. ARMA outlines a few exceptions to these rules. If you have a particular
     situation that requires an exception to a rule, refer to ARMA rules. To simplify this
     section, all the exceptions have not been included. The following rules have been
     organized into three categories:
            A. Personal Names.
            B. Business and Organization Names.
            C. Government and Political Designations.

 A. Specific Filling Rules for Personal Names

     initial, and then the middle name or initial. Because nothing comes before something,
     initials (such as M) are filed before a name (such as Martha) that begins with the same
     letter.

    4. Preparing Paper Records for Filing
   Once you decide which records are to be managed, you must establish a procedure for
    preparing the records to be filed. The following six steps will help you prepare your
    records for filing:

         1.   Examine
         2.   Index
         3.   code
         4.   Cross-reference
         5.   Sort
         6.   File

1. Examine:

        You will want to make certain the item is ready for filing. Follow these guidelines to
         examine each item
        Check items that are stapled together and decide if they should be filed together.
        Staple together related paper where record refers to the other
        Remove all paper clips and extra staples as these supplies use extra space and can
         hang on other records.
        Remove small slips of paper that are no longer needed, such as a slip marked “Please
         file.”
        Keep the routing slip with the appropriate documents. You may need to determine
         later to whom the document was circulated.
        Determine if document are duplicates and should be deleted/destroyed or files.

2. Index
    In a previous section, you learned indexing occurs when you determine the order and
    format of the units in a name when a alphabetizing. Careful indexing is the most
    important part of filing.

    When you are filing correspondence by name, note the following:


    --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Page 21 of 34                                                                                   By: Wail Nassar Badah
    Office Management II                                                           Chapter 1: Record Management
    --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

         a. Incoming letters are often called for by the name of the organization appearing in
            the letterhead, so index them by the name of the organization in the letterhead.
         b. Outgoing letters are often called for by the name of the organization appearing in
            the inside address, so index them any the name of the organization in the inside
            address.

    To learn how to index for the variety of names you will encounter, follow the
       standardized indexing rules presented earlier in this chapter.

3. Code
    After you decide how a record should be filed, mark the indexing caption on it. This
    process is called coding. To code by name, highlight, underscore, circle, or write the
    indexed name.

4. Cross-Reference
    When a document is apt to be called for by two different names at different times, you
    should be able to locate it by looking under either name. to make this possible, file the
    record according to the caption (the name arranged in index order) by which it is most
    likely to be requested. Also, file a reference to it by a second name.

5. Sort
    Using the code marks mentioned previously as a guide, prearrange the records in the
    same order in which they will eventually be filed. This preliminary arrangement is called
    sorting. By sorting the records carefully, you will eliminate unnecessary shifting back
    and forth, from drawer to drawer or form shelf to shelf, as you file them. As a result, you
    will be working efficiently and saving time.

        Horizontal and vertical sorters can expedite the process; these devices, such as trays
         and booklets, use dividers that are labeled either alphabetically or numerically, to sort
         records.

6. File
    After records are sorted, they are ready to be filed. Filing is the actual placing of records
    in folders. It involves classifying, arranging, and storing materials according to a
    systematic plan for quick reference. Here are a few tips to follow to help you in handling
    your filing tasks:

   When refilling existing records, the name on the caption in the filing system should be
    the same as the records being refilled.

    6. Electronic Filing

    Electronic records are being created at an increasing rate. As the number of computer
    files increases, the need to organize these files is more important than ever.

6.1.      Types of Electronic Files

    A. FILE
    In most ways, an electronic filing system works much like a paper filing system. Put
     simply, in an electronic filing system you have cabinets that contain file folders that
     contain files, also called documents. In an electronic filing system, a file is any
     document such as a spreadsheet, database, report, picture, or presentation.
    B. FOLDER
    A directory in an electronic system contains files. You can create as many folders
     and subfolders as you need; however, be mindful of the number or layers of
     subfolders. Folders organize the files in a logical manner, like using manila folders
     inside hanging folders to organize paper documents in file cabinet drawers.

    C. DRIVE
    The drive is the physical location where folders and files can be found. A drive name
     consists of a single letter and a colon.

‫االحتفاظ بها لديها‪ ،‬باستخدام نموذج استبعاد وإيداع الوثائق اإلدارية لترحلها إلى األرشيف أو‬
                                                                            ‫اتخاذ قرار بشأن استهالكها‪.‬‬
‫يقوم مدراء اإلدارات بمراجعة هذا البيان والتأكد من عدم الحاجة لهذه الوثائق أو التعديل في البيان‬                     ‫‪‬‬
‫سواء بالحذف أو اإلضافة والتوقيع بإمضائهم على نموذج استبعاد وإيداع الوثائق اإلدارية من‬
                                                                                           ‫أصل وصورة‪.‬‬
‫تقوم سكرتارية كل إدارة بإرسال أصل وصورة من نموذج استبعاد وإيداع الوثائق اإلدارية إلى قسم‬                           ‫‪‬‬
                            ‫األرشيف‪( .‬الملحق رقم ‪ 6‬الخاص بترحيل الوثائق إلى قسم األرشيف)‪.‬‬
‫بالنسبة للمستندات المالية وأوراق الصرف والفواتير فيتم استبقاءها في دائرة الشؤون المالية لمدة‬                       ‫‪‬‬
‫عامين تحت طلب الجهات الرقابية‪ ،‬وبعدها تحول إلى تحول إلى األرشيف بناء على الئحة مدد‬
                                                                                                   ‫الحفظ‪.‬‬
‫يقوم الموظف المختص باألرشفة (المساعدة اإلدارية) باتخاذ إجراءاته لتنفيذ ما ورد في اللوائح‬                           ‫‪‬‬
                               ‫والنماذج الواردة من اإلدارات بالتنسيق مع لجنة تقييم الوثائق اإلدارية‪.‬‬


                                                              ‫القواعد العامة للترحيل واالستهالك‬                     ‫‪5-2‬‬
‫عند وضع الئحة تبن الفترة الزمنية الخاصة بالوثائق اإلدارية (مدد الحفظ) فإنه من الضروري‬
‫مراعاة بعض القواعد التي يتم على أساسها ترحيل األوراق واستهالكها ونعرض هنا بعض هذه‬
                                                                                                  ‫القواعد‪:‬‬
                                                                  ‫أن بعض األوراق تفقد أهميتها تلقائيا‪.‬‬             ‫‪‬‬
‫أن أساس الترحيل هو مدى التوسع في استخدام األوراق تبعا للفترة الزمنية ومدى الحاجة للرجوع‬                            ‫‪‬‬
                                                                                                     ‫إليها‪.‬‬
‫كلما طال عمر األوراق فإنه من الضروري النظر في ترحيلها‪ ،‬أما التخلص من األوراق فال يجب‬                               ‫‪‬‬
‫أن يكون أساسه هو عمرها إنما الفصل في ذلك هو مدى الحاجة إليها وأهميتها بالنسبة ألعمال‬
                                                                                                ‫المؤسسة‪.‬‬
             ‫التعليمات القديمة التي صدرت تعليمات حديثة بإلغائها يجب النظر في التخلص منها‪.‬‬                          ‫‪‬‬

‫الحق أو التقادم وفقا للقوانين العامة التي تحدد المدة التي يتم بعدها التخلص منها‪ ،‬أما األوراق‬

‫تشكل لجنة تسمى بلجنة تقييم الوثائق اإلدارية وتعقد سنوياً في األسبوع الثالث من شهر‬

                                                                                       ‫المستشار القانوني‬          ‫‪‬‬
                                                                            ‫مدراء إدارات إدارة المؤسسة‪.‬‬           ‫‪‬‬
‫ويرأس أعمال هذه اللجنة المدير المالي وهو الذي يحدد بمذكرة منه إلى اإلدارات مواعيد ومكان‬                           ‫‪‬‬
                                                                                   ‫انعقاد جلسات اللجنة‪.‬‬
                                                                                    ‫مهام لجنة تقييم الوثائق اإلدارية‪:‬‬
       ‫فحص بيانات نموذج استبعاد وإيداع الوثائق اإلدارية المقدمة من اإلدارات والتصديق عليها‪.‬‬                       ‫‪‬‬
‫النظر في جدول االستبقاء‪ ،‬واقتراح تعديل مدد استبقاء بعض أنواع الوثائق‪ ،‬والموافقة على ما تراه‬                       ‫‪‬‬
                                                                                            ‫صالحا منها‪.‬‬
‫النظر في اإلضافات الذي يقدمها قسم األرشيف أو الصادر والوارد أو مدراء اإلدارات بتحديد مدد‬                          ‫‪‬‬
            ‫استبقاء‪ ،‬أو استبعاد لنوعيات الوثائق اإلدارية التي لم ترد في الجداول وتقييمها وإقرارها‪.‬‬
                                                                                                    ‫‪ .6‬الوثائق السرية‪-:‬‬
‫أن جميع الوثائق اإلدارية الواردة و الصادرة أو المحفوظة هي معلومات سرية ال يجب اطالع أي شخص‬
‫من غير المختصين عليها ‪ ،‬وهذا احتياط عام تقتضيه المحافظة على سرية العمل وبوجه عام فإن بعض‬
‫الوثائق اإلدارية لها صفة سرية خاصة وهذه يجب أن يتحفظ عليها‪ ،‬وتعطي عناية خاصة خالل تداولها‬
                                                                                         ‫وحفظها واإلطالع عليها ‪.‬‬

                ‫كذلك المعلومات التي تتعلق بموضوعات سرية في نطاق إحدى اإلدارات أو األقسام‪.‬‬
‫السري جدا‪ :‬وهي المعلومات التي تتعلق بموضوعات يضر إفشاء سريتها بصالح إدارة الجمعية‪،‬‬                                  ‫‪‬‬
                                              ‫وذلك مثل المشروعات التي تكون تحت الدراسة و البحث‪.‬‬
‫السري للغاية‪ :‬وهي المعلومات التي تتعلق بموضوعات يضر إفشاء سريتها بصالح العام للسلطة‬                                 ‫‪‬‬
                                ‫الوطنية الفلسطينية مثل أمور المفاوضات و االتفاقيات وما شابه ذلك‪.‬‬
